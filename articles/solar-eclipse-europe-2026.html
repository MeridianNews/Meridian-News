Europe's First Total Solar Eclipse in 27 Years Happens Today

Aug 12, 2026 · 4 min read

A total solar eclipse is sweeping across the far north of Europe today, August 12, marking the continent's first since 1999. The Moon's shadow is racing from the Russian Arctic over the North Pole, across Greenland and Iceland, and down into northern Spain before the day is out.

Unlike most eclipses, which travel roughly west to east, this one arcs up and over the pole before curving back down toward the Iberian Peninsula. It begins at an unusual local "sunrise" over Russia's Taymyr Peninsula, where the sun never fully sets in August, before the shadow crosses open ocean toward Greenland and Iceland.

Fact check: Around 15 million people live within the path of totality, while nearly 980 million — about one in eight people worldwide — are able to see at least a partial eclipse across Europe, North Africa and parts of North America.

Where and when to watch

Totality reaches Greenland and Iceland in the late afternoon and early evening, with Reykjavik experiencing its first total eclipse since 1433. It then crosses the Atlantic before arriving in northern Spain around 8:30pm local time, low on the western horizon, marking Spain's first total solar eclipse in more than a century.

Major cities will see a partial eclipse

Madrid and Barcelona sit just outside the path of totality but are still seeing a deep partial eclipse. Much of Western Europe, including London and Paris, is seeing the sun significantly covered even without experiencing full darkness, with London reaching close to its maximum coverage in the early evening.

Safety first

Skywatchers are reminded that the sun must never be viewed directly without certified eclipse glasses or filters, except during the brief seconds of totality itself when the sun is fully blocked. Outside the path of totality, only a partial eclipse is visible, meaning proper eye protection is required throughout.
