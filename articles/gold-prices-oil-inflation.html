Gold Nears Two-Month High as Oil Prices and Inflation Fears Rise

Aug 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Gold climbed close to a two-month high this week, trading above $4,400 an ounce, as investors turned to safe-haven assets amid surging oil prices and growing uncertainty over the pace of US interest rate cuts.

The rally comes as crude oil holds near multi-week highs, with Brent crude trading close to $90 a barrel. Higher energy costs are stoking concerns about renewed inflationary pressure, which in turn is making investors and central bankers more cautious about how quickly borrowing costs might come down.

Fact check: Spot gold has risen more than 8% over the past week alone, adding over $300 an ounce as demand for safe-haven assets accelerated. Gold remains well below its late-January 2026 record high, having fallen nearly 30% before this recent rebound.

Who's buying, and why

Central banks, particularly in Asia, have been steady buyers of gold in recent months. China's central bank added to its reserves again in July, extending a streak of monthly purchases, while Chinese institutional investors have continued building gold-backed fund positions as a hedge against volatility elsewhere in their portfolios.

What's driving oil higher

Energy markets remain on edge over the unresolved standoff between the US, Israel and Iran, along with ongoing uncertainty about when — or whether — shipping through the Strait of Hormuz will fully return to normal. Elevated crude prices tend to raise costs across transport and manufacturing, feeding into broader inflation expectations.

What investors are watching next

Markets are awaiting fresh US inflation data this week, including the Consumer Price Index for July, which could shape expectations for the Federal Reserve's next move on interest rates. A hotter-than-expected inflation reading could further support gold's safe-haven appeal, while a cooler print may ease some of the recent volatility across both energy and precious metals markets.
