Iran and Oman Near Deal on New Strait of Hormuz Shipping Routes

Aug 11, 2026 · 4 min read

Iran and Oman are in the final stages of agreeing on new shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz, a development that could mark a step toward reopening one of the world's busiest energy corridors after months of disruption.

Officials from both countries say they have settled on the geographical coordinates for inbound and outbound routes through the strait, with a joint statement reportedly in its final drafting stage. Talks have continued for more than three weeks and have also covered technical arrangements, sovereignty concerns for both coastal nations, and a proposed joint coordination center to monitor maritime traffic.

Fact check: Shipping through the strait has been sharply reduced since fighting between the US, Israel, and Iran broke out in late February 2026. Traffic fell from roughly 130 vessels a day before the conflict to as few as 8 on a recent day, according to reporting cited by regional outlets.

What the deal covers

Under the emerging arrangement, temporary shipping lanes near Iran's Larak Island and through Omani waters would be retired in favor of new routes running largely through Iranian territory, with sections passing through Omani waters. The new routes are expected to remain in place for two to four months, possibly longer, while a more permanent framework is worked out.

Why this doesn't end the crisis

Iranian officials have been careful to note that a bilateral understanding with Oman would not, by itself, reopen the strait to normal international traffic. Washington's involvement remains a separate and unresolved question, and Tehran has ruled out direct talks with the United States for now, pointing to what it describes as repeated violations of a short-lived ceasefire agreement reached in June. Iran says it is instead communicating with Washington through intermediaries.

Adding to the uncertainty, Iran recently reshuffled its top security leadership, appointing a hardline former commander to head its Supreme National Security Council. The move has raised questions among analysts about which direction Tehran's diplomacy will take in the weeks ahead.

What happens next

A finalized Iran-Oman statement is expected soon, but analysts caution it would represent a technical first step rather than a full resolution. Broader questions — including fees for vessels transiting the strait, sanctions relief, and Iran's nuclear program — remain unresolved between Tehran and Washington. For now, global shipping and energy markets are watching closely for signs that traffic through one of the world's most critical chokepoints may begin returning to normal.
